Systems Thinking & Quality Management in Airport Operations
Course Project | Quality Management
Overview
This is a quality management course assignment that applies systems thinking (feedback loops) and quality management principles to analyze complex airport operations. It explains how to stabilize daily services, ensure safety, and drive sustainable growth through systematic logic and practical cases.
Core Concepts: Feedback Loops
Balancing Loop (Stabilizer)
Maintains stability and corrects deviations to meet targets (e.g., security wait time ≤ 15 mins, flight punctuality).
Reinforcing Loop (Growth Engine)
Amplifies changes into a self-reinforcing spiral: positive cycles drive growth; negative cycles trigger collapse.
Case Studies
1. Balancing Loop: Stable Security Check
Goal: Keep average wait time ≤ 15 minutes
Process: Queue lengthens → wait time hits 20 mins → open standby lanes + deploy extra staff → restore stability
2. Reinforcing Loop: Aviation Hub Growth (Positive Cycle)
Efficient transfer → smooth experience → positive word-of-mouth → more passengers → new airlines/routes → higher revenue → reinvest in facilities → further improve service
3. Reinforcing Loop Risk: Uncontrolled Negative Spiral
Storm → flight cancellations → gate congestion → taxiway delays → staff overload → frequent errors → more delays → service collapse → public complaints
Key Quality Tool
PDCA Cycle (Plan–Do–Check–Act)
Closed-loop management for continuous improvement in airport safety, service, and operations.
Strategic Takeaways
Use balancing loops to secure service quality and safety baselines.
Leverage reinforcing loops to drive business growth and brand value.
Prevent negative spirals by avoiding system overload.
Apply PDCA for long-term quality sustainability.
